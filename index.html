1. The problem

Classrooms keep running after students leave

What problem am I solving?

Classroom ACs, tube lights and fans stay on after students leave, and nobody measures how much or for how long.

Who is affected and why does it matter?

KCC (about 300 rooms) pays for the extra electricity, everyone shares the extra carbon, and no one is responsible for switching off.

What inspired me?

Seeing it myself in my own classrooms at KCC while doing a Campus Sustainability Assessment, and hearing the same concern from a classmate.
31.5 to 54 kWh a dayEstimated waste (assumed): 30 of about 300 rooms each leave one AC on for an extra hour. That is 4.6 to 7.9 tonnes of CO2 a year.
2. Insights and research

What I found out

Observed on campus

On 8 Oct 2026 I photographed a classroom with AC, lights and fans on and a washroom tap running. A classmate, Pratyaksh Pandey, raised both issues.

Made it measurable

I turned the problems into 9 indicators, such as rooms left on while empty, AC running in an empty room, and kWh wasted per day.

Practised the data skills

I cleaned and charted a public air-quality dataset (116 days). Lesson: averages hide the extreme days.

Key insight: one AC-hour equals about 14 to 36 fan-hours of energy, and nobody measures it. So measure and switch off the AC first, and watch the worst rooms, not just the average.
3. The AI-enabled solution

KCC SwitchSmart

The system switches off the AC, fans and lights when a room is empty and no class is due, and uses AI to spot waste that a fixed rule cannot predict.

Sensorspresence, power, timetable
Controllerswitch-off rule in each room
AI modelanomaly score for each room
Outputalerts and dashboard

Key features

Presence sensing that detects people, not bags. Timetable-aware switch-off after 15 empty minutes, with a 1-hour override button. Energy metering for each room. AI alerts with a plain reason and a dashboard of the 10 worst rooms.

How AI is used

Isolation Forest anomaly detection learns what normal power use looks like for each room, hour and day, then flags unusual windows. It needs no labelled examples, which KCC does not have yet.

Tools and AI used

Python, pandas and scikit-learn for the simulation. Proposed hardware: ESP32 controller, mmWave presence sensor, energy meter. Dashboard in Looker Studio, alerts by Telegram bot. I used Claude (Anthropic AI assistant) to help structure the work and write code and documents. The KCC observations and photos are my own.

5. Prototype testing and results

What the simulation showed

How I tested

Simulated 10 rooms for 28 days (about 27,000 time windows). The AI learned from days 1 to 14 and was tested on days 15 to 28 with faults I added.

Results

Caught 3 of 3 overnight AC faults and 6 of 6 "AC left on after class" events. Flagged 75 of 13,100 normal windows (0.57%) by mistake.

What went wrong

A faulty AC using 30% extra power during class was almost missed (1.4%).
Learning and next fix: one model for all rooms cannot see a room that is slightly worse than normal, so version 2 will compare each room with its own history and with similar rooms. Honest limit: these are simulated results. They show the logic works, not that KCC will save energy. A classmate confirmed the problem, but real users have not tried the system yet.

Pilot: weeks 1 to 8

10 rooms vs 10 similar rooms. Goal: 50% less waste, 2 or fewer complaints a month.

Expansion: months 3 to 6

About 100 rooms. Goal: 70% of AI flags confirmed real by staff, alert owner named.

Full-scale: months 7 to 12

All about 300 rooms, then labs and offices. Monthly report to the department head.
Main challenges: AC cutting off during a lecture (timetable plus presence, override button); sensors missing seated students (mmWave sensors, tested in the pilot); alerts ignored (one named owner).
